# Stock Fundamentals Scanner - Evaluation Methodology

## Overview
Our stock scanner uses a **multi-layered validation approach** to identify high-quality stocks with strong fundamentals and real upside potential. We don't rely on any single metric - instead, we combine quantitative analysis, external validation, and risk assessment to surface genuinely promising opportunities.

## Core Philosophy

**What We Believe:**
1. **Quality over hype** - Strong fundamentals beat stories
2. **Multi-factor validation** - No single metric tells the whole story
3. **Risk-adjusted returns** - Downside protection matters as much as upside
4. **Transparency** - Show our work, including weaknesses
5. **Continuous improvement** - Track results and refine

**What We Avoid:**
- Value traps (cheap but deteriorating)
- Growth at any price (high growth, negative cash flow)
- Over-leveraged companies (high debt risk)
- Low-quality earnings (accounting tricks)
- Illiquid stocks (hard to exit)

## The 5-Pillar Scoring Framework

### 1. Financial Health (25% weight)
**What it measures:** Can this company weather storms?

**Key Metrics:**
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio**: Can they service debt?
  - <50: Excellent (conservative balance sheet)
  - 50-100: Good (manageable debt)
  - 100-200: Acceptable (monitor closely)
  - >200: High risk (vulnerable to rate shocks)

- **Free Cash Flow**: Are they generating real cash?
  - Positive FCF with >70% conversion: Excellent
  - Positive FCF: Good
  - Operating cash flow positive: Acceptable
  - Cash burn: Red flag

- **Liquidity Ratios** (Current/Quick): Can they pay bills?
  - >1.5: Strong cushion
  - >1.0: Adequate
  - <1.0: Liquidity concerns

**Why it matters:** Companies with poor financial health can collapse even with "good" metrics. Debt crises, cash crunches, and liquidity issues can wipe out shareholders overnight.

---

### 2. Profitability (25% weight)
**What it measures:** Is this a sustainable business?

**Key Metrics:**
- **Profit Margins**: Operating leverage and pricing power
  - >20%: Excellent (strong moat)
  - 10-20%: Good (competitive business)
  - 5-10%: Acceptable (thin margins)
  - <5%: Concerning (vulnerable to competition)

- **Return on Equity (ROE)**: Capital efficiency
  - >20%: Exceptional (compounding machine)
  - 15-20%: Strong (good capital allocation)
  - 10-15%: Acceptable (average business)
  - <10%: Weak (poor returns)

- **Operating Margins**: Core business profitability
  - Consistent or expanding: Positive
  - Contracting: Warning sign

**Why it matters:** Unprofitable or low-margin businesses struggle to create value. High ROE companies compound wealth faster.

---

### 3. Growth (20% weight)
**What it measures:** Is the business expanding?

**Key Metrics:**
- **Revenue Growth** (YoY):
  - >25%: High growth
  - 15-25%: Strong growth
  - 10-15%: Good growth
  - 5-10%: Moderate growth
  - <5%: Low/no growth
  - Negative: Declining business

- **Earnings Growth**: Bottom-line expansion
  - Compared to revenue growth: Quality check
  - Faster than revenue: Margin expansion (good)
  - Slower than revenue: Margin compression (concern)

**Why it matters:** Growth creates optionality. But growth without profitability is unsustainable. We want PROFITABLE growth.

---

### 4. Valuation (15% weight)
**What it measures:** Are we overpaying?

**Key Metrics:**
- **P/E Ratio**: Price relative to earnings
  - <15: Potentially undervalued
  - 15-25: Fair value range
  - 25-40: Priced for growth
  - >40: Rich valuation (little margin for error)

- **PEG Ratio**: P/E relative to growth
  - <1.0: Attractive (growth at reasonable price)
  - 1.0-2.0: Fair
  - >2.0: Expensive relative to growth

- **Price-to-Sales, Price-to-Book**: Alternative views
  - Lower is generally better
  - Compare to industry averages

**Why it matters:** Even great companies can be bad investments at the wrong price. Valuation determines your margin of safety and potential returns.

---

### 5. Leverage/Debt Management (15% weight)
**What it measures:** Financial prudence and risk

**Scoring:**
- D/E <30: Excellent (1.0 score)
- D/E 30-50: Very good (0.9)
- D/E 50-75: Good (0.7)
- D/E 75-100: Acceptable (0.5)
- D/E 100-150: Concerning (0.3)
- D/E >150: High risk (0.1)

**Why it matters:** Debt amplifies both gains and losses. High-debt companies are vulnerable to:
- Rising interest rates
- Economic downturns
- Refinancing risk
- Covenant violations

## External Validation Layers

### Layer 1: Analyst Consensus
**What we check:**
- Consensus recommendation (1.0 = Strong Buy, 5.0 = Strong Sell)
- Number of analysts covering (more = better)
- Price target upside potential

**Red flags:**
- Our score says "buy" but analysts say "sell" → Flag for review
- Very few analysts (<3) → Lacking institutional attention
- Huge disconnect between our score and consensus → Investigate

**Why it matters:** Professional analysts have resources we don't. Major disagreement warrants caution.

---

### Layer 2: Price Action Context
**What we check:**
- **52-Week Range Position**:
  - Near 52-week low (-20% or more): Potential value opportunity OR value trap
  - Mid-range: Neutral
  - Near 52-week high (+5% or less): Momentum OR overheated

- **Volume Analysis**:
  - Declining volume + falling price: Bearish
  - Rising volume + rising price: Bullish
  - Unusual volume surge: Investigate catalyst

**Why it matters:** Fundamentals tell you WHAT to buy. Technicals tell you WHEN to buy. We want both aligned.

---

### Layer 3: Quality Filters
**Minimum Requirements:**
- **Market Cap**: >$1 billion (avoid micro-caps with liquidity issues)
- **Average Daily Volume**: >500K shares (ensure liquidity)
- **Data Completeness**: Must have at least 60% of key metrics
- **Positive Free Cash Flow**: Preferred (cash is king)

**Why it matters:** These filters eliminate:
- Penny stocks susceptible to manipulation
- Illiquid stocks hard to exit
- Companies with incomplete/unreliable data

## Quality Tiers Explained

### Excellent (Score: 80-100)
**Characteristics:**
- Strong across ALL 5 pillars
- Low debt, high profitability, growing revenue
- Reasonable valuation with analyst support
- Minimal weaknesses

**Investment Approach:** Core holdings, can be aggressive on position sizing

**Historical Success Rate Target:** 70%+ beat market over 6 months

---

### Good (Score: 65-79)
**Characteristics:**
- Strong in 3-4 pillars
- 1-2 areas of concern but manageable
- Generally solid fundamentals

**Investment Approach:** Quality holdings, standard position sizing

**Historical Success Rate Target:** 60%+ beat market over 6 months

---

### Fair (Score: 50-64)
**Characteristics:**
- Mixed fundamentals
- Some strengths, some notable weaknesses
- May be turnaround candidates or cyclical plays

**Investment Approach:** Selective, smaller positions, more active management

**Historical Success Rate Target:** 50%+ (coin flip - needs catalyst or timing)

---

### Poor (Score: <50)
**Characteristics:**
- Weak fundamentals across multiple pillars
- High debt, low profitability, or declining business
- May be value traps

**Investment Approach:** Avoid or watch only (unless contrarian play with specific thesis)

**Historical Success Rate Target:** Not recommended for most investors

## Risk Assessment Framework

### Risk Levels
**Low Risk:**
- Score >70 AND debt/equity <75 AND positive FCF
- Strong balance sheet provides downside protection

**Moderate Risk:**
- Score 50-70 OR some financial health concerns
- Standard volatility expected

**High Risk:**
- Score <50 OR debt/equity >200 OR negative FCF
- Vulnerable to unexpected shocks

### Risk Factors We Flag
1. **Financial distress indicators**:
   - High debt-to-equity (>150)
   - Negative free cash flow
   - Current ratio <1.0

2. **Business deterioration**:
   - Declining revenue
   - Contracting margins
   - Negative earnings growth

3. **Valuation risk**:
   - P/E >50 (little room for error)
   - PEG >3.0 (overpaying for growth)

4. **Analyst concerns**:
   - Consensus "sell" rating
   - Downward target revisions

## How We Ensure Accuracy

### 1. Pre-Launch Validation
- **Backtest against known winners**: Test against stocks that performed well historically
- **Backtest against known losers**: Ensure we filter out companies that collapsed
- **Industry validation**: Compare our scores to widely-respected fundamental analysts

### 2. Post-Launch Tracking
**Performance Metrics We'll Monitor:**
- **30/60/90-day returns** by quality tier
- **Win rate**: % of stocks that outperform market
- **Drawdown protection**: Do our stocks fall less in downturns?
- **Surprise rate**: % that have negative surprises (earnings miss, guidance cut)

**Success Criteria:**
- Excellent tier: 70%+ outperform SPY over 6 months
- Good tier: 60%+ outperform SPY over 6 months
- Fair tier: 50%+ (neutral, useful for specific strategies)

### 3. Continuous Refinement
**Monthly Reviews:**
- Analyze false positives (high scores that failed)
- Analyze false negatives (low scores that succeeded)
- Adjust weights and thresholds based on data

**Quarterly Enhancements:**
- Add new factors if patterns emerge
- Remove/downweight factors that don't predict
- Update industry-specific adjustments

### 4. Transparency & User Feedback
- Show detailed breakdown of scores
- Explain WHY each stock is rated
- Allow users to see our track record
- Incorporate user feedback on misses

## Limitations & Honest Disclosure

### What This Scanner Can't Do
1. **Predict black swan events** (fraud, pandemics, regulatory shocks)
2. **Time the market perfectly** (fundamentals don't tell you exact entry/exit)
3. **Replace due diligence** (still read 10-Ks, understand business)
4. **Guarantee returns** (no system is perfect)

### What Users Should Still Do
1. **Read earnings reports** - understand the business
2. **Check recent news** - we don't analyze headlines in real-time
3. **Diversify** - don't put everything in one "Excellent" stock
4. **Set stop losses** - manage risk actively
5. **Monitor positions** - fundamentals can deteriorate

### Known Blind Spots
- **Sector rotations** - we don't predict macro shifts
- **Management quality** - hard to quantify leadership
- **Competitive threats** - we see current metrics, not future disruption
- **Accounting quality** - we use reported numbers (can be manipulated)

## Example: How We Evaluate a Stock

**Stock: ABC Corp**

**Step 1: Gather Data**
- yfinance pulls all fundamental metrics
- Analyst consensus data
- Price history and volume

**Step 2: Calculate Component Scores**
- Financial Health: 0.85 (strong balance sheet, positive FCF)
- Profitability: 0.75 (good margins, solid ROE)
- Growth: 0.60 (moderate revenue growth)
- Valuation: 0.50 (fairly valued, P/E of 22)
- Leverage: 0.90 (low debt)

**Step 3: Calculate Overall Score**
- Weighted average: (0.85×0.25) + (0.75×0.25) + (0.60×0.20) + (0.50×0.15) + (0.90×0.15) = 0.73
- Overall Score: 73/100 → **Good** tier

**Step 4: Validate Externally**
- Analyst consensus: 2.1 (Buy)
- Price target upside: 18%
- ✅ Aligned with our score

**Step 5: Risk Assessment**
- Debt/equity: 45 (low)
- Free cash flow: $500M (positive)
- Current ratio: 1.8 (strong)
- **Risk Level: Low**

**Step 6: Identify Strengths & Weaknesses**
- **Strengths**: Strong balance sheet, consistent profitability, positive FCF
- **Weaknesses**: Growth slowing, valuation not cheap
- **Buy Reason**: "Quality compounder with strong cash generation"

**Step 7: Present to User**
- Clear overall score and tier
- Detailed metric breakdown
- Strengths/weaknesses listed
- Risk factors noted
- Recommendation with reasoning

**The goal:** Beat the market over time by avoiding disasters and compounding in quality stocks.

**The promise:** Full transparency, continuous tracking, honest assessment of what works and what doesn't.

**The commitment:** Refine the methodology based on real results, not theory.
